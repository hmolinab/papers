## Abstract

We consider the gradient flow $\dot\Gamma=-\nabla P(\Gamma;\mu,J)$ on the real $4\times4$ matrices, with
$P=\|\Gamma\|^2+\mu\det\Gamma+\beta\|\Gamma\|^4+b_6\|\Gamma\|^6-\langle J,\Gamma\rangle$, and its damped
second-order version. The field-free potential is invariant under the bilateral orthogonal action
$\Gamma\mapsto U\Gamma V^\top$ ($U,V\in O(4)$, $\det U\det V=1$). The ring of invariants of this action is
generated by the symmetric functions of the singular values and by the determinant; the latter is the only
orientation-sensitive generator, and the naive candidate for a cubic invariant, $\operatorname{tr}\Gamma^3$, is
not invariant under this symmetry. Near a Hessian degeneracy—a simple soft mode, isolated by the linear term
$J$ in the sense of imperfect bifurcation—centre-manifold reduction yields the local normal forms, and the
cubic coefficient receives from the determinant, through its cofactor matrix, its unique orientation-sensitive
contribution; within the potential considered it is its only source on the stratum $V\perp\Gamma_*$. We prove
the codimension-1 reduction (fold and, on strata with $\mathbb Z_2$ isotropy, pitchfork) with explicit
coefficients, and the codimension-2 cusp $A_3$. A no-Hopf lemma shows that no metric-gradient flow, for any
positive-definite metric, admits an oscillatory bifurcation: the spectrum of the degeneracy is real. The same
mechanism, under the conjugation symmetry $O(n)$ of symmetric tensors, selects $\operatorname{tr}Q^3$, as in the
Landau–de Gennes energy of the nematic $Q$-tensor. Each statement is accompanied by reproducible numerical
verification.

Keywords: equivariant bifurcation, matrix gradient flow, centre-manifold reduction, normal forms, cofactor
matrix, orientation invariant, no-Hopf lemma, cusp.

## 1. Introduction

Bifurcation theory organizes the qualitative changes of a dynamical system around a catalogue of normal forms
(Guckenheimer and Holmes 1983; Kuznetsov 2004). When the field has a symmetry, equivariant theory (Golubitsky,
Stewart and Schaeffer 1988) determines which bifurcations are generic, why degeneracies appear in families, and
which invariants may enter the normal form. We study, for a gradient flow with a matrix variable and bilateral
orthogonal symmetry, the origin of the cubic term of the soft-mode bifurcation.

The content is that the structure of the invariant ring of the symmetry fixes that term. The determinant is the
only generator of the bilateral ring that is not a function of the tensor $\Gamma^\top\Gamma$, that is, the only
orientation-sensitive one; upon projecting onto the centre manifold it supplies the orientation part of the
cubic. The third-degree invariant in eigenvalues, $\operatorname{tr}\Gamma^3$, does not belong to the bilateral
ring. The same logic, for the conjugation symmetry of symmetric tensors, selects $\operatorname{tr}Q^3$: it is
the cubic of Landau–de Gennes theory (§7). The result of this work is the bilateral instance of that pattern.

We prove the codimension-1 reduction with explicit coefficients (§3–§4), classify the codimension-2 cusp (§5),
establish a no-Hopf lemma that delimits the scope of the gradient sector (§6), place the mechanism alongside its
conjugation counterpart (§7), and verify each result numerically (§8).

## 2. The system and its symmetry

Let $V=M_4(\mathbb R)\cong\mathbb R^{16}$ with the Frobenius inner product
$\langle X,Y\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(X^\top Y)$. We take
$$
P(\Gamma;\mu,J)=\|\Gamma\|^2+\mu\det\Gamma+\beta\|\Gamma\|^4+b_6\|\Gamma\|^6-\langle J,\Gamma\rangle,\qquad \beta,b_6\ge0,
$$
with control parameter $\mu$ and external field $J\in M_4(\mathbb R)$. The sextic $b_6$ does not enter the local
statements; it stabilizes globally the subcritical branches when the slaving correction (Theorem 1.4) turns the
effective quartic negative.

For $J=0$, $P$ is invariant under $\Gamma\mapsto U\Gamma V^\top$ of the group
$G=\{(U,V)\in O(4)\times O(4):\det U\det V=1\}$, since $\|U\Gamma V^\top\|^2=\|\Gamma\|^2$ and
$\det(U\Gamma V^\top)=\det\Gamma$. This is the singular-value-decomposition symmetry.

The polynomial invariants of $G$ are functions of the singular values $\sigma_i$ of $\Gamma$: they are generated
by the power sums $p_k=\operatorname{tr}((\Gamma^\top\Gamma)^k)=\sum_i\sigma_i^{2k}$, all of even degree and
functions of $\Gamma^\top\Gamma$, and by the determinant $\det\Gamma=\pm\prod_i\sigma_i$, with $\det^2$ already
expressible in the $p_k$. The determinant is thus the only generator that is not a function of
$\Gamma^\top\Gamma$; it is the orientation-sensitive invariant. The naive candidate for a cubic,
$\operatorname{tr}\Gamma^3=\sum_i\lambda_i^3$ (powers of the eigenvalues), is not $G$-invariant: under
$(U,V)\in G$, $\operatorname{tr}(U\Gamma V^\top)^3=\operatorname{tr}(U\Gamma(V^\top U)\Gamma(V^\top U)\Gamma V^\top)$,
which depends on $V^\top U$ and reduces to $\operatorname{tr}\Gamma^3$ only when $V=U$. It lies outside the
bilateral ring. Only under the conjugation subsymmetry $U=V$ would it be invariant, which is the case of
symmetric tensors (§7).

The continuous symmetry would make the degeneracies appear in $G$-orbits rather than in isolation. The linear
term $-\langle J,\Gamma\rangle$ breaks $G$. Generically it breaks it completely: it intersects the $G$-orbits
transversally (Thom transversality), lifts the degeneracy, and leaves a single zero eigenvalue of the Hessian
(hypothesis H2). This is the theory of imperfect bifurcations (Golubitsky and Schaeffer 1985). Since $J$ enters
linearly, it displaces the equilibrium without altering the Hessian. If $J$ preserves an isotropy subgroup
$\mathbb Z_2\subset G$ (a field on a symmetric stratum), the soft mode inherits that isotropy and the generic
bifurcation is the pitchfork (§3); pitchforks live on the symmetric strata, as is standard in equivariant
bifurcation theory.

The gradient flow is
$$
\dot\Gamma=-\nabla P,\qquad \nabla P=2\Gamma+\mu\,\mathrm C(\Gamma)+\big(4\beta\|\Gamma\|^2+6b_6\|\Gamma\|^4\big)\Gamma,
$$
with $\mathrm C(\Gamma)=\operatorname{cof}(\Gamma)=\partial\det\Gamma/\partial\Gamma$. The Hessian $H(\Gamma)=D^2P$
is symmetric. We also consider the damped second-order version $\ddot\Gamma+\gamma\dot\Gamma+\nabla P=0$, whose
limit $\gamma\to\infty$ recovers the gradient flow; only to probe basins and escape rates (§8) do we use the
Langevin extension $\dot\Gamma=-\nabla P+\sqrt{2D}\,\eta$.

Remark 2.1 (scope and dimension). Parts 1–2 of Theorem 1 are the centre-manifold / Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction
for any real-analytic $P$ under (H1)–(H2); only the identification of the cubic coefficient uses the form of $P$.
The construction requires a positive-definite inner product (the Frobenius one), which bounds $P$ from below and
fixes the gradient structure. We present $n=4$ for concreteness and because there $\det$ has degree $4$ and
$D^3\det$ is linear; the mechanism extends to $M_n(\mathbb R)$ with the bilateral action $O(n)\times O(n)$, where
$\det$ has degree $n$. A Monte Carlo sampling over $(\beta,b_6,J)$ confirms the persistence of the simple soft
mode and of the determinant's contribution (§8).

Remark 2.2 (other fourth-degree invariants). The bilateral ring contains a second degree-four invariant besides
$\det$ and $\|\Gamma\|^4=p_1^2$: namely $p_2=\operatorname{tr}((\Gamma^\top\Gamma)^2)$. Its cubic contribution is
$D^3p_2[V,V,V]=24\langle\Gamma_*,V(V^\top V)\rangle$, which is not a function of $\langle\Gamma_*,V\rangle$ and
therefore, unlike that of $\|\Gamma\|^4$, does not vanish in general on $V\perp\Gamma_*$. We do not include $p_2$
in $P$: we work with the potential above, where on $V\perp\Gamma_*$ the determinant is the only source of the
cubic. The statement that survives with $p_2$ present is weaker but intrinsic: the determinant is the only
orientation-sensitive contribution to the cubic, since $p_2$, being a function of $\Gamma^\top\Gamma$, is blind
to orientation.

## 3. Main result (codimension 1)

At $(\Gamma_*,\mu_*)$ we assume: (H1) $\nabla P=0$; (H2) $H_*=H(\Gamma_*)$ has a simple zero eigenvalue, with
unit eigenvector $V$, and the rest of the spectrum bounded away from zero; (H3) transversality
$\tau:=\langle V,\mathrm C(\Gamma_*)\rangle\neq0$; (H4) non-degeneracy $a_3:=D^3P(\Gamma_*)[V,V,V]\neq0$, except
on strata with $\mathbb Z_2$ isotropy, where $a_3=0$ and $a_4^{\mathrm{eff}}\neq0$.

Theorem 1 (reduction $\Gamma\to\xi$). Under (H1)–(H2) there exists, in a neighbourhood of $(\Gamma_*,\mu_*)$, a
unique smooth one-dimensional centre manifold $\Gamma=\Gamma_*+\xi V+h(\xi,\mu)$ with $h\in V^\perp$,
$h=O(\xi^2,\xi\tilde\mu)$, on which the flow is gradient, $\dot\xi=-\partial_\xi\Phi(\xi,\mu)$. If in addition
(H3)–(H4):

1. if $a_3\neq0$, the reduced form is the fold $\dot\xi=\alpha-\tfrac12 a_3\xi^2$, with $\alpha=-\tau(\mu-\mu_*)$;
2. if a $\mathbb Z_2$ isotropy forces $a_3=0$, the reduced form is the pitchfork
   $\dot\xi=-a_2'\xi-\tfrac16 a_4^{\mathrm{eff}}\xi^3$;
3. $a_3=\mu_*\,D^3\!\det(\Gamma_*)[V,V,V]+24\beta\langle\Gamma_*,V\rangle$; the second term vanishes on the
   stratum $V\perp\Gamma_*$ (the set of equilibria $\Gamma_*$ orthogonal to their soft mode $V$ in the
   Frobenius inner product, $\langle V,\Gamma_*\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(V^\top\Gamma_*)=0$), where the
   determinant is the only source of the cubic within $P$. The determinant's
   contribution is, moreover, the only orientation-sensitive one (Remark 2.2);
4. $a_4^{\mathrm{eff}}=D^4P[V^{\otimes4}]-3\,\langle D^3P[V,V],(H_*|_{V^\perp})^{-1}D^3P[V,V]\rangle$; its sign is
   fixed by the spectrum of $H_*|_{V^\perp}$.

In the symmetric sector, $a_4^{\mathrm{eff}}$ changes sign at $\mu=16\beta$, the line separating the regime of
one equilibrium from that of three (Figure 1).

## 4. Proof (Lyapunov–Schmidt)

This is the Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction for gradient fields (Carr 1981; Golubitsky and Schaeffer 1985). With
$\Gamma=\Gamma_*+\xi V+W$, $W\in V^\perp$, $\mu=\mu_*+\nu$, and $Q$ the projection onto
$V^\perp=\operatorname{ran}H_*$, the component $Q\nabla P=0$ is solved by the implicit function theorem
($H_*|_{V^\perp}$ invertible by H2) and yields the slaving $W=h(\xi,\mu)$, with $\partial_\xi h(0,\mu_*)=0$ and
$\partial_\xi^2 h(0)=-(H_*|_{V^\perp})^{-1}Q\,D^3P(\Gamma_*)[V,V]$. Substituting, the bifurcation equation
$g=\langle V,\nabla P\rangle$ coincides, since the field is gradient, with $\partial_\xi\Phi$. With
$a_k=\partial_\xi^k\Phi(0,\mu_*)$: $\partial_\nu a_1|_0=\langle V,\mathrm C(\Gamma_*)\rangle=\tau$;
$a_2(\mu_*)=\langle V,H_*V\rangle=0$; $a_3=\langle V,D^3P[V,V]\rangle$, with no slaving correction since it is
$\perp V$.

We decompose $D^3P$ by terms. The $\|\Gamma\|^2$ term is quadratic and does not contribute. For the quartic norm,
$D^3(\|\Gamma\|^4)[V,V,V]=24\langle\Gamma_*,V\rangle$. For the determinant, of degree $4$, $D^3\det$ is linear and
$D^3(\mu\det)[V,V,V]=\mu_*\,D^3\!\det(\Gamma_*)[V,V,V]$. Summing gives point 3. On $V\perp\Gamma_*$ the norm term
vanishes and only the determinant term survives.

The geometric reading is direct in the singular values: $\det\Gamma=\prod_i\sigma_i$ couples the four
irreducibly and supplies the orientation-sensitive part; $\|\Gamma\|^2=\sum_i\sigma_i^2$ is a function of
$\Gamma^\top\Gamma$ and enters only through $\langle\Gamma_*,V\rangle$. The effective quartic follows from
differentiating $g$ once more; its sign is fixed by the definiteness of $H_*|_{V^\perp}$. The fold/pitchfork
classification follows from Sotomayor's conditions (1973): with $a_1(\mu_*)=a_2(\mu_*)=0$ and $a_3\neq0$ one
obtains the fold; if a $\mathbb Z_2$ annuls $a_3$, the first nontrivial term is the quartic and one obtains the
pitchfork. $\square$

The full symbolic computation is in the Supplementary Material (`code/`) and in the technical document
`teorema_gamma_xi.md`, §2.

## 5. Codimension-2 cusp

Theorem 2 (cusp $A_3$). A point with $a_2=a_3=0$ and $a_4^{\mathrm{eff}}\neq0$ is a cusp, the universal unfolding
of the pitchfork (Thom 1975). Since the contributions of the determinant and of the norm compete, $a_3(\mu,s)$
changes sign and, by the intermediate value theorem, vanishes on a curve; the second control $s$ fixes $a_2=0$
at a point of that curve, where $a_4^{\mathrm{eff}}\neq0$ and the transversality
$\partial(a_1,a_2)/\partial(\mu,s)$ is nonsingular, so that the $A_3$ is versal. The four conditions are
certified numerically (§8); the window of three equilibria scales as $(-a_2)^{3/2}$ (Figure 2).

## 6. A no-Hopf lemma

Lemma 1 (no Hopf in the metric-gradient flow). At an equilibrium $\Gamma_*$, the Jacobian of
$\dot\Gamma=-G^{-1}\nabla P$, for any symmetric positive-definite metric $G\succ0$ (including a
state-dependent Riemannian metric $G(\Gamma)$; at $\nabla P(\Gamma_*)=0$ the term with the derivative of
the metric vanishes), has real spectrum. No complex pair crosses the imaginary axis and no Hopf bifurcation
occurs: while the dynamics is metric-gradient, the soft mode admits only stationary bifurcations (fold,
pitchfork, cusp). An oscillatory instability requires breaking the gradient form: either $G$ losing
positivity, or a non-gradient reactive term.

*Proof.* The Jacobian at $\Gamma_*$ is $J=-G_*^{-1}H_*$, with $G_*=G(\Gamma_*)\succ0$ and $H_*=D^2P(\Gamma_*)\in\mathrm{Sym}$. Write
$J=G_*^{-1/2}\bigl(-G_*^{-1/2}H_*G_*^{-1/2}\bigr)G_*^{1/2}$;
since $G_*^{-1/2}H_*G_*^{-1/2}$ is symmetric, its spectrum is real, and similarity preserves the spectrum.
Hence $\sigma(J)\subset\mathbb R$ for every $G_*\succ0$ and $H_*\in\mathrm{Sym}$. $\square$

The lemma organizes the catalogue by the spectrum of the degeneracy. A simple real eigenvalue crossing zero
gives a stationary bifurcation, with the cubic originating from the determinant (Theorems 1–2). An oscillatory
regime requires a complex pair crossing the axis, impossible in the gradient sector. The type is read off the
critical spectrum.

The lemma admits a thermodynamic reading: strict dissipation, as a gradient flow with a positive metric, is
obstructed from producing sustained oscillation or limit cycles. It is the dynamical correlate of why a system
in monotone relaxation harbours no self-sustained rhythm. In Onsager's framework (1931), the purely dissipative
sector (symmetric Onsager matrix) admits no oscillatory currents; oscillatory instabilities require the
antisymmetric reactive contribution, exactly as Lemma 1 requires a non-gradient term. Unlike Theorems 1–2, the lemma does not depend on the
particular potential or on the dimension.

## 7. The conjugation counterpart: the $Q$-tensor

The mechanism of §3 has a counterpart under another symmetry. For the conjugation action $Q\mapsto RQR^\top$,
$R\in O(n)$, on symmetric tensors, the invariant $\operatorname{tr}Q^3$ is indeed invariant (it is a function of
the eigenvalues, which conjugation preserves). In the Landau–de Gennes energy of nematic liquid crystals,
$$
F(Q)=\tfrac a2\operatorname{tr}Q^2-\tfrac b3\operatorname{tr}Q^3+\tfrac c4(\operatorname{tr}Q^2)^2,
$$
it is $\operatorname{tr}Q^3$ that supplies the cubic responsible for the first-order isotropic–nematic
transition (de Gennes and Prost 1993; Mottram and Newton 2014). Thus, depending on the symmetry, the
lowest-degree invariant that breaks isotropy changes: the determinant for the bilateral action,
$\operatorname{tr}Q^3$ for conjugation. The result of this work is the bilateral instance; the conjugation one is
known physics. We do not claim a general theorem encompassing both; the observation is that the cubic of the
normal form is supplied, in each case, by the lowest-degree anisotropic invariant admitted by the symmetry, and
it should be verified case by case. $Q$-tensors and continuum strain tensors are real matrix gradient flows and
offer a testing ground.

## 9. Discussion

When the nonlinearity of a matrix gradient flow with bilateral symmetry is fixed by that symmetry, the
orientation-sensitive invariant, the determinant, determines the type of soft-mode bifurcation through its
cofactor. That $\operatorname{tr}\Gamma^3$ lies outside the bilateral ring is what distinguishes the determinant
without recourse to a choice of potential: $P$ uses the admitted generators of lowest degree. The determinant
enters as the invariant that generates the orientation part of the cubic, not as an indicator of stability; that
is fixed by the spectrum of the symmetric Hessian. Lemma 1 delimits the scope of the gradient sector.

On the $3/2$ exponent. The window of three equilibria of the cusp scales as $(-a_2)^{3/2}$. This is the universal
law of the $A_3$ catastrophe, shared by any cusp, and by itself does not validate the determinant mechanism;
what it validates is that the organizer is a genuine cusp. The measurable number is the signature of the class of
bifurcation, not of the invariant that originates it.

Where these flows appear. Real matrix gradient flows arise in the Landau–de Gennes $Q$-tensor (§7), continuum
strain tensors, and the loss landscapes of small neural networks, where an invariant nonlinearity governs the
soft mode. In adversarial training (GANs) the gradient symmetry is broken natively by the min-max structure,
exciting the oscillatory modes that Lemma 1 attributes to the non-gradient sector.

Scope note. We do not claim this Lagrangian is privileged: it is one of the class with bilateral symmetry, built
from the admitted generators of lowest degree. The word "universal" is used only in the technical sense of
universal unfolding of normal forms. The determinant generates the orientation part of the cubic within this
class, not as a statement about arbitrary matrix flows.

Directions, treated in the companion document as exploration and not as theorems: the second-order inertial
extension, where the Jordan block of Bogdanov–Takens is released (obstructed in the gradient limit by Lemma 1),
with its coefficient certificate in $16+16$ dimensions open; the phenomenology of chaos (saddle-focus,
Shilnikov) of the non-gradient sector; and the extension to $M_n(\mathbb R)$, $n>4$.
